Cashless Hospital Finder — the question a patient actually asks

“Where can I have this surgery done, near me, without paying upfront?” Insurer network lists are PDFs organised by city; hospital directories are filed by medical department; neither is organised the way anyone asks, so the lead-capture form standing in for an answer was losing 80% of the users who reached it. I replaced it with pincode or city plus treatment and insurer, returning hospitals ranked by distance, rating and insurance panel — then the doctors at that hospital who perform the specific procedure. The hard part was neither maps nor ranking: patients search for a condition, hospitals are filed by department, and “Piles” appears nowhere in a network that calls it General Surgery. I built the prototype myself to prove that mapping worked before committing an engineering sprint.

~50 leads/day · ~30 surgeries/month · ₹21 lakh booked value monthly

How I work

Four habits

  • Discovery before sizing. The Policy Decoder exists because of a sentence that kept coming up in user conversations — "I don't know what my company's policy covers" — that no dashboard would have surfaced.
  • Smallest thing that produces a real signal. A no-code CMS beat a proper CMS build by a quarter. A self-built prototype beat waiting for a sprint slot — twice, and both are in production now. Cutting a rule engine down to one eligibility check saved seven engineering days and lost nothing.
  • Say no in writing. Every deferral in my specs carries a reason. The decisions-made and questions-open register is the part of a PRD I care most about — specs don't stall because people disagree, they stall because nobody remembers what was already agreed.
  • Instrument before launch. I build the dashboards, so I don't ship what I can't read.
